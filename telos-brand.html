T-ELOS

τέλος — the final end of a purpose. The name is a machine that closes loops, so the design language writes itself: lines that travel, then stop decisively. The hyphen is the most ownable thing on the page — it's the one letterform Vivo's "Telos" doesn't have. Four directions, from armour to philosophy.

TELOS

01 · The Nail Hyphen

The hyphen becomes the bronze nail — the Talos myth: the single pin that seals the lifeblood. A rivet through the word. Tradesmen know what a nail is. It welds the myth, the game, and the job site into one 2-millimetre detail.

02 · The Terminus

A line that almost circles — then stops hard in a filled point. That's telos: the end where the process is complete. The unfinished circle says "the loop is open"; the point says "not on my watch." The mark is the product.

03 · The Tau

Greek letter τ — the T of Telos, the old sign of protection and completion. Crossbar up top, then a bronze stroke closing the foot: the baseline "sealed." Inscription feel without the toga. This one holds a crest or stamp beautifully.

04 · The Full Stop

The quiet, expensive one — old-style serif, wide tracking, and a bronze full stop after the word. The period is the whole idea: the end of the process, done. Tagline locks the meaning for anyone who missed Greek class. Family with Operator's "Serif" DNA.
